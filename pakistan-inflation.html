What it means

CPI inflation measures the change in the average price level of a basket of consumer goods and services compared with the same period a year earlier. A 5% annual rate means the measured basket is about 5% more expensive than a year earlier, although individual household spending can change by more or less.

Why it matters

Inflation affects household purchasing power, business costs, interest rates and the value of savings. PakDigitalDashboard also tracks the Sensitive Price Indicator (SPI) for a shorter-term view of selected everyday prices.

How to compare inflation rates

The national CPI year-on-year rate is a broad measure, not a statement that every household experienced the same percentage change. Spending patterns, cities, income levels and individual prices differ. For that reason, PakDigitalDashboard identifies the national CPI measure and reporting month instead of presenting it as a personal cost-of-living rate.

How the CPI measure is read

The national consumer price index is a broad statistical measure. The year-on-year rate compares the index with the corresponding period a year earlier. It should not be interpreted as a promise that every household experienced the same percentage change, because household baskets differ.

How it is measured

The Pakistan Bureau of Statistics compiles the CPI from prices for a defined basket of goods and services. The important context is the observation month, geographic coverage and statistical definition supplied with the release. PakDigitalDashboard therefore keeps the release month beside the headline number and links to PBS rather than treating the dashboard as the original statistical authority.

Why it matters for readers

Inflation can change the purchasing power of income and savings and can affect business input costs. It is also one input into broader economic analysis. A single monthly observation is best read alongside its recent history and other indicators rather than in isolation.

Frequently asked questions

Does CPI mean every household's costs rose by this rate?

No. CPI is a statistical basket measure. Individual households have different spending patterns, so their experienced price changes can differ.

Why is the reporting month important?

The rate describes a specific observation period. Comparing different months without stating the period can create a misleading picture of change.

Where should I verify the figure?
