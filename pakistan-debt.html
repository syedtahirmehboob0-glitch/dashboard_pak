How the Rs 57.566T domestic public debt is structured

The Pakistan Economic Survey 2025-26 reports the end-March 2026 domestic public-debt stock at about Rs 57.6 trillion. Its component breakdown is:

  • Permanent debt: Rs 43.857T — mainly Pakistan Investment Bonds, Government Ijara Sukuk and Prize Bonds.
  • Floating debt: Rs 9.583T — mainly Market Treasury Bills.
  • Unfunded debt: Rs 3.215T — including National Savings instruments and Prize Bonds.
  • Other: Rs 0.910T — including Naya Pakistan Certificates, SDR on-lending and foreign-currency domestic debt.

These components total approximately Rs 57.565T because of rounding, matching the reported Rs 57.6T domestic-debt stock.

Broader debt-and-liabilities profile

For a different purpose, SBP's end-December 2025 debt-and-liabilities profile reported a broader stock of Rs 95.464T. The detailed categories include government domestic and external debt, IMF obligations, PSE debt, private-sector external debt, external liabilities, commodity operations and intercompany liabilities.

  • Government domestic debt: Rs 55.363T
  • Government external debt: Rs 23.166T
  • IMF debt: Rs 2.845T
  • Private-sector external debt: Rs 5.520T
  • Public-sector enterprises external debt: Rs 2.200T
  • PSE domestic debt: Rs 2.068T
  • External liabilities: Rs 3.383T
  • Commodity operations: Rs 1.086T
  • Intercompany external debt: Rs 1.538T
Important: the Rs 95.464T broader measure is not a second way of adding up the Rs 83.285T public-debt headline. The measures use different definitions, dates and consolidation treatment. The listed SBP categories can also overlap for analytical purposes, so they should not be mechanically summed.

How to read Pakistan's debt figures

Debt figures are not interchangeable. Public debt, government debt and broader debt-and-liabilities measures can use different definitions, consolidation rules and reporting dates. PakDigitalDashboard keeps those measures separate so a visitor can trace each number to its source rather than combining incompatible totals.

How to interpret the debt breakdown

The public-debt headline and the broader debt-and-liabilities measure on this page are deliberately kept separate. They come from different institutional publications and reporting dates, and their definitions are not interchangeable. This prevents readers from treating two different accounting scopes as if they were duplicate measurements of one stock.

Why domestic and external debt are separated

Domestic and external public debt describe where the relevant obligations are classified within the reporting framework. The page keeps the source's categories and dates visible so that the component figures are not detached from their original definition.

How it should be used

Debt figures are most useful when read with their date, definition and component structure. The Ministry of Finance and SBP publications are the authoritative places to verify the underlying releases. PakDigitalDashboard adds a readable layer and links back to those sources.

Frequently asked questions

Is the broader Rs 95.464T figure the same as public debt?

No. The page explicitly separates the broader debt-and-liabilities measure from the Mar-2026 public-debt headline because the definitions and dates differ.

Why do component totals sometimes differ by a small amount?

Published components can be rounded. The page notes where the displayed components approximately reconcile to the reported headline.

Which source should I use for an official figure?

Use the Ministry of Finance or State Bank of Pakistan publication linked in the Sources section for the relevant definition and date.
