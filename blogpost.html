How to read Pakistan's public debt number

Debt explainer · Reviewed 3 October 2026

A public-debt headline is useful only when you know exactly what it includes and when it was measured. Pakistan's official publications distinguish public debt from broader debt-and-liabilities measures, and those measures can have different reporting dates.

The Ministry of Finance's Debt Bulletin for the first half of FY2026 reported public debt of Rs 81.374 trillion at end-December 2025, with 68% domestic and 32% external. The Pakistan Economic Survey later reported domestic public debt of about Rs 57.6 trillion at end-March 2026. These figures are not contradictory simply because the dates differ; a debt stock is a point-in-time measure.

Another common mistake is adding categories from different tables. A broader debt-and-liabilities framework can include items such as IMF obligations, public-sector enterprise debt and other liabilities that are not simply another presentation of the public-debt headline.

Practical rule: before comparing two debt numbers, write down three things: definition, reporting date and unit. If any of those differ, treat the figures as different measures rather than automatically calculating a change.

What Pakistan's CPI inflation rate actually tells you

Inflation explainer · Reviewed 3 October 2026

When you see a headline such as “Pakistan inflation,” the first question should be: which inflation measure and which period? The national Consumer Price Index is a weighted statistical measure of changes in prices across a defined basket. It is not a promise that every household's personal expenses rose by exactly the same percentage.

The Pakistan Bureau of Statistics reported national CPI inflation of 10.26% year-on-year in September 2026 and 1.27% month-on-month. The same release explains that national CPI combines urban and rural measures using weights. That means a household's experience can differ depending on its spending pattern, location and the prices it actually pays.

Year-on-year and month-on-month rates answer different questions. YoY compares September 2026 with September 2025 and is useful for seeing the change over a full year. MoM compares September with August and can show shorter-term movement. Neither number alone describes every price in the economy.

PakMetric therefore labels the month and measure on its Pakistan inflation page. The primary source is the Pakistan Bureau of Statistics monthly inflation report.

USD to PKR: why the reference rate is not your transaction rate

Exchange-rate explainer · Reviewed 3 October 2026

USD/PKR is often quoted as one number, but there are several rates that can be relevant. An official or market reference rate helps describe the rupee's value against the US dollar; a bank, exchange company or other provider may quote a different customer rate because of spreads, fees, market conditions and the type of transaction.

This distinction matters when estimating the rupee value of an overseas payment or the cost of an imported item. A reference rate is useful for analysis, while the rate you actually receive or pay should come from the provider handling the transaction.

For example, the PakMetric page currently identifies its displayed figure as an SBP M2M revaluation reference rather than presenting it as a universal cash-market price. That label is deliberate: the same currency can have different quoted rates for different purposes.

Quick check: when comparing USD/PKR figures, record the date, source, rate type and whether fees or spreads are included.

Why worker remittances matter to Pakistan

External-sector explainer · Reviewed 3 October 2026

Worker remittances are money sent to Pakistan by overseas workers and families. For the economy, the important point is that remittances are a flow: the amount reported for August is money recorded during August, not the total amount held by overseas Pakistanis.

SBP's EasyData series describes total cash worker remittances as a monthly USD flow. Its August 2026 observation was about US$3.656 billion. Monthly numbers can move because of timing, seasonality and other factors, so a single month should be interpreted in the context of a longer series.

Remittances can matter to recipient households through income and consumption, while at the macro level they contribute to foreign-currency inflows. They are one part of Pakistan's external accounts and should be considered alongside exports, imports, reserves and the current account rather than treated as a standalone measure of economic health.

How to read Pakistan petrol-price data

Fuel-price explainer · Reviewed 3 October 2026

Fuel-price searches often mix together several concepts: a notified petroleum price, a daily or district/pump quote, a historical notification and the price a particular customer ultimately pays. Those are not necessarily identical.

OGRA's petroleum-price pages publish notifications and related price information with effective dates. As of 3 October 2026, OGRA's notified-price page lists a petroleum-products notification effective 3 October 2026. Its portal also publishes daily fuel-price information.

For a consumer or business, the source date matters because fuel prices can change. A delivery operator estimating monthly costs should not use an undated screenshot from an old notification. A researcher comparing months should record the effective date and the exact product being compared.

Better comparison: compare the same fuel product, use the same type of price, and align the effective dates. Do not treat a pump-specific figure and a national notification as interchangeable.

How to read a Pakistan economic dashboard without mixing dates

Data-literacy guide · Reviewed 3 October 2026

A dashboard is useful because it puts many indicators together, but that convenience can create a hidden problem: the indicators rarely update on the same schedule. Inflation may be monthly, remittances monthly, debt at a point in time, exchange rates daily, and market data much more frequently.

The safest way to read a dashboard is to treat every number as a pair: value + date/definition. Then add a third piece of information: the source. This prevents a reader from accidentally comparing a September inflation figure with a December debt stock and assuming they describe the same moment.

PakMetric uses source dates, definition notes and direct institutional links for this reason. The Pakistan economy guide provides a map of the major indicators, while the individual pages provide more detail.

A good dashboard should also show uncertainty honestly. A live feed can temporarily fail; a statistical release can later be revised; and two institutions can publish figures with different methodologies. Those are reasons to label the data carefully, not reasons to hide the limitations.

Three-question habit: What exactly is being measured? When was it measured? Who published it? If you can answer those three questions, you are much less likely to misread an economic headline.
