SBP Keeps Pakistan’s Policy Rate at 11.5%: What the Decision Means

The State Bank kept the policy rate unchanged in September 2026. Here is what the decision means and what data to watch next.

The decision

SBP says its Monetary Policy Committee kept the policy rate at 11.5% at its September 14, 2026 meeting. Its published dashboard also shows a 12.5% overnight reverse-repo ceiling and 10.5% floor.

Why it matters

The policy rate influences financial conditions, but it is not the same as the rate on a personal loan or deposit. Banks also apply benchmarks, spreads, fees and repricing terms.

What to watch

SBP’s calendar schedules the September inflation monitor for no later than October 5 and September remittance data for no later than October 9.

Reader takeaway

Compare the effective rate and full terms of any financial product rather than assuming every rate moves one-for-one with the policy rate.

The policy rate is one part of the transmission chain

The State Bank's policy rate is an important signal for financial conditions, but households and businesses do not all borrow at exactly that rate. Banks price loans using their own funding costs, benchmark structures, credit risk, fees and contractual terms. Deposit returns can also respond differently and with a lag. The practical effect of a policy decision therefore depends on how quickly financial institutions pass changes through to customers.

An unchanged rate is also a policy decision. It can indicate that the monetary authority is maintaining the existing stance while waiting for more information on inflation, external conditions, demand and other indicators. The correct way to interpret an unchanged decision is to read the accompanying monetary-policy statement and compare the reasons given there with the data that were available at the meeting date.

What businesses should watch

Companies with floating-rate borrowing can be affected when benchmark rates change, while firms with fixed-rate financing may see no immediate change. Interest-sensitive sectors can therefore respond differently from exporters, commodity producers or companies whose revenues are less dependent on domestic credit conditions. The aggregate market response can be equally mixed because expectations about future policy often matter before an actual rate change occurs.

For savers, the effect depends on the product and repricing schedule. A policy-rate move does not guarantee a one-for-one change in every deposit rate. Readers should compare the effective annual return, fees and lock-in terms of an actual product instead of using the policy rate as a proxy for a consumer rate.

What to monitor next

Track the next inflation release, external-account data, exchange-rate conditions and the wording of the next monetary-policy statement. Looking at these series together provides more context than interpreting the policy rate in isolation.
