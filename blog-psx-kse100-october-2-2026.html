Pakistan Stock Exchange: What Happened to the KSE-100 on October 2, 2026

A dated look at the latest KSE-100 session and the market factors reported at the time.

The session

PSX data showed the KSE-100 around 168,806.75 in an October 2 snapshot, while reporting on the completed Friday session put the close at 168,155, down 481 points or 0.29%.

Market context

Reports pointed to subdued participation, elevated crude-oil prices and geopolitical uncertainty. Selling was reported across several sectors while some oil and gas names provided support.

Why oil matters

Pakistan imports much of its energy, so global oil prices can affect the import bill, inflation pressure and business costs.

Reader takeaway

An index is a benchmark, not a forecast. One trading session should be read alongside company fundamentals, earnings and longer periods of market data.

Reconciling the session figures

Two numbers in a market story can appear contradictory when they describe different moments of the same trading day. A live or intraday market snapshot can show a level that differs from the official closing index. For the October 2 session, the article should therefore distinguish the intraday or published snapshot from the completed-session close rather than presenting both as if they were the same observation.

The closing level is the appropriate figure when describing the final result of the trading session. An intraday value can still be useful for showing how the index moved during the day, but it needs an explicit timestamp or description. This distinction is particularly important for search readers who may encounter a headline after the market has closed and assume the first number they see is the official close.

What moves an index

The KSE-100 is a weighted market index, so the performance of large constituents can have a disproportionate effect on the headline. Sector composition matters: banks, oil and gas companies, fertilizer producers, technology businesses and other large constituents can respond to different earnings, commodity, policy and macroeconomic signals. A falling index does not mean every listed company fell, just as a rising index does not mean every share gained.

Daily trading can also reflect liquidity, foreign flows, profit-taking, corporate announcements and changes in risk sentiment. These factors can interact with domestic inflation, interest rates, the rupee and global commodity prices. The sensible approach is to treat one session as a dated market observation rather than a standalone explanation of the economy.

How to read the next session

Use the official PSX market summary for the closing level, percentage change and trading statistics. Then compare the move with sector performance and major company announcements. Longer windows are more useful for identifying trends, while a single day is best treated as a snapshot.
