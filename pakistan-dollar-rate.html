Why the dollar rate matters

A higher USD/PKR number means more rupees are needed to buy one US dollar. This can raise the rupee cost of imported fuel, machinery and other foreign goods. It can also increase the rupee value of dollar-denominated remittances and overseas freelance income.

Reference rate vs. the rate you receive

USD/PKR reference data is useful for tracking the rupee, but it should not be confused with a cash-market, bank or exchange-company customer quote. Transaction rates can include spreads, fees and different market conditions. Always check the applicable provider rate before exchanging money.

Reference rate, market rate and transaction rate

A published USD/PKR reference number is useful for tracking the rupee, but it is not automatically the rate a person receives from a bank, exchange company or cash-market transaction. Those rates can reflect provider spreads, fees and market conditions. The observation date is therefore as important as the number itself.

How it is measured

The reference shown here is identified as an SBP M2M revaluation reference. That makes it a standardized reference for the stated date rather than a guarantee of a retail transaction price. Readers comparing rates should use the same date, quote type and unit.

Why it matters

USD/PKR is relevant to imported goods, foreign-currency obligations and the rupee value of dollar-denominated inflows. It is also a useful market indicator when read alongside inflation, reserves, remittances and external-account data.

Frequently asked questions

Is the reference rate the rate I will get at a bank?

No. A reference rate and a customer transaction quote serve different purposes. Check the applicable provider quote before exchanging money.

Why can two USD/PKR rates differ?

They may represent different markets, times, transaction types or spreads. Always compare like-for-like quotations.

What date does the dashboard show?

The page identifies the observation date next to the headline reference value so readers can distinguish it from a later market quote.

Why is a reference rate different from a bank quote?
