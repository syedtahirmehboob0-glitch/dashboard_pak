Why worker remittances matter to Pakistan

External-sector explainer · Reviewed 3 October 2026

Worker remittances are money sent to Pakistan by overseas workers and families. For the economy, the important point is that remittances are a flow: the amount reported for August is money recorded during August, not the total amount held by overseas Pakistanis.

SBP's EasyData series describes total cash worker remittances as a monthly USD flow. Its August 2026 observation was about US$3.656 billion. Monthly numbers can move because of timing, seasonality and other factors, so a single month should be interpreted in the context of a longer series.

Remittances can matter to recipient households through income and consumption, while at the macro level they contribute to foreign-currency inflows. They are one part of Pakistan's external accounts and should be considered alongside exports, imports, reserves and the current account rather than treated as a standalone measure of economic health.
