How to read Pakistan's public debt number

Debt explainer · Reviewed 3 October 2026

A public-debt headline is useful only when you know exactly what it includes and when it was measured. Pakistan's official publications distinguish public debt from broader debt-and-liabilities measures, and those measures can have different reporting dates.

The Ministry of Finance's Debt Bulletin for the first half of FY2026 reported public debt of Rs 81.374 trillion at end-December 2025, with 68% domestic and 32% external. The Pakistan Economic Survey later reported domestic public debt of about Rs 57.6 trillion at end-March 2026. These figures are not contradictory simply because the dates differ; a debt stock is a point-in-time measure.

Another common mistake is adding categories from different tables. A broader debt-and-liabilities framework can include items such as IMF obligations, public-sector enterprise debt and other liabilities that are not simply another presentation of the public-debt headline.

Practical rule: before comparing two debt numbers, write down three things: definition, reporting date and unit. If any of those differ, treat the figures as different measures rather than automatically calculating a change.
